## Philosophy reading list

* 📚 Laozi. (~500 BC). [🆓 道德经](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E9%81%93%E5%BE%B7%E7%B6%93) (Tao Te Ching, public domain). _Why:_ A short classic on restraint, systems thinking, and leading without forcing—useful when research complexity tempts over-control.
* 📚 Plato. (~380 BC). [🆓 Republic](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1497) (理想国, public domain). [Cornell University Library](https://www.library.cornell.edu/). _Why:_ It begins with a deceptively simple question—what is justice?—and trains you to test definitions, examine assumptions, and argue with clarity.
* 📚 Ludwig Wittgenstein. (1921). [🆓 Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/5740) (TLP, 逻辑哲学论, public domain). [Cornell University Library](https://www.library.cornell.edu/). _Why:_ A rigorous lesson in the limits of language and logic—how far formal systems can say, and where they must fall silent.
* 📚 Bertrand Russell. (1948). [🆓 Human Knowledge: Its Scope and Limits](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.461169) (人类的知识, public domain). [Cornell University Library](https://www.library.cornell.edu/). _Why:_ A clear map of what we can know, how we justify it, and where certainty ends—handy for framing research claims honestly.

## Standard citation managers

* 🧩 [EndNote](https://endnote.com/)
  * Access at Cornell: see [Cornell Library — Citation Management](https://www.library.cornell.edu/study/citation-management/) (site licenses for CALS & Human Ecology; otherwise trial/purchase via EndNote). [LibGuide](https://guides.library.cornell.edu/citations/software)
  * [Tutorials, FAQs & videos](https://clarivate.libguides.com/endnote_training/users)
  * For “Find Full Text,” add Cornell’s OpenURL in preferences: `http://resolver.library.cornell.edu/net/openurl/`
  * Cite While You Write works with MS Word; pick the journal style (APA, IEEE, Elsevier, ACS/MDPI, etc.) in EndNote
  * For an organization/government document, use the short name as Author (corporate) and full name as Publisher in a Report-type reference. E.g.,
    * NIBS. (2015). _National Building Information Modeling Standard. Version 3_, Washington, DC, USA: National Institute of Building Sciences (NIBS). Retrieved from https://www.nationalbimstandard.org/ (accessed on 1 Jan 2020).
  * Prefer Report-type entries for webpages and online documents when a formal bibliographic style fits better than a bare “Web Page”
  * Article number (ASCE and some Elsevier journals) can go in the Pages field
  * Set a DOI (or URL) on every reference entry
* 🧩 [LaTeX](http://course1.winona.edu/eerrthum/LaTeX/Windows.html)
  * 💽 [BibTeX](http://www.bibtex.org/Using/) for thesis, books, and as a citebase
  * 💇 A clean BibTeX `.bib` for each article
  * Note the formatting control macros, e.g., `{}`, `--`, `$$`
  * Set a DOI (or URL) to every reference entry
